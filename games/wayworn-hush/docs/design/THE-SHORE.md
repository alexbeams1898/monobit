# Wayworn Hush — The Shore (source material)

> Raw material for authoring the shore, drawn from the real place it is based on
> (Crab Meadow, on the Long Island Sound). Kept short on purpose — these are
> seeds, not a setting bible. Nothing here is player-facing text.
>
> The place is never named in game and the real-world reference is never stated.

## The three that matter

**The sun leaves the water in winter.** The beach faces north. Midsummer the sun
sets far north of west — out over the water, down the Sound's long axis. By
midwinter it sets southwest, *behind the bluffs, over land*: the winter shore
loses its sunset entirely. The light returns to the water as the year opens and
leaves it as the year closes. A seasonal arc that needs no writing on top of it.

**The settlement gave the name up; the shore kept it.** Everything here was once
called by the shore's name. The town renamed itself and moved on; the beach still
carries what the whole place used to be called. A shoreline holding an older name
than the place it belongs to.

**Shells underfoot, and shells six feet down.** Middens behind the beach — 75 feet
across, 6 feet deep, centuries of shellfish eating compacted into a stratum of
shell in the ground. And the same shells crunching underfoot on the beach today.
The same sound, a thousand years apart, in one place.

## The ground

Rocky, not soft — cobble-and-sand, water-shoes rocky. The stones are glacial
erratics carried from the north, so they are *many different rocks*, not one
local stone: granite, gneiss, quartz, schist, rounded and sorted by the water.
Boulders drop onto the beach as the sandy bluffs erode. Sandbars and shallow
tidal pools at low tide. Calm water — a sound, not an ocean, so no surf.

Low evening sun rakes *along* the beach lengthwise rather than toward it, so the
cobbles and the wrack line throw long shadows at that hour and the wet sandbars
mirror.

## What washes up

- **Jingle shells** — thin, translucent, iridescent; orange, yellow, white, grey.
  Named for the sound a handful makes when shaken.
- **Slipper shells** — arched and boat-like with an internal shelf. They stack in
  towers; all start male and become female, largest at the bottom.
- **Hard clam** — thick and chalky white with a purple interior margin.
- **Knobbed whelk egg case** — a twisted string of yellowish discs, feet long,
  tan and papery when dried. Looks like a snake left on the sand.
- **Moon snail sand collar** — a rubbery grey ring of cemented sand. An egg case
  that looks like a discarded object.
- **Skate egg case** — black, leathery, squarish, stamp-sized, with curling straps.
- **Also:** surf clam, bay scallop, mud snails, sea star, moon jelly (clear
  saucer), lion's mane jelly (stings), driftwood, beach glass, feathers, reed
  stems off the marsh.

## The marsh behind it

Tidal creeks and mud flats; long straight ditches cut a century ago to drain
mosquitoes, which became highways for crabs that burrow in and chew the roots —
an old repair that turned into the wound. Tall reed stands rattle in wind and go
straw-gold and dead-standing through winter. Salt hay lies down in swirled
cowlicks. Glasswort — succulent, jointed — turns fiery red in autumn, the best
colour note in the marsh year. Ribbed mussels studded through the mud, each in
its burrow. Fiddler crabs, the males with one oversized claw, ticking over the
mud at low tide.

The low-tide smell is sulfur off the exposed mud. The water sound is *drainage* —
creek and ditch — not surf.

## Living things

Osprey (returning in spring, on the nest), great egret, great blue heron,
diamondback terrapin, plovers, sandpipers, gulls. A rail heard far more often
than seen. **Winter-only:** long-tailed duck and red-throated loon offshore —
these *are* the visual signature of the cold months.

**Horseshoe crabs spawn in May and June** — at night, at high tide, within a few
days of the full or new moon. Bodies of them at the tideline in the dark. Given
what the shore is named for, the richest seasonal event available.

## Season

- **Spring** — horseshoe crabs on the moon tides, osprey back, marsh greening.
- **Summer** — warm knee-deep sandbars, jellies later on, the sun setting into
  open water.
- **Autumn** — glasswort reddens, reed goes straw and plumes, birds moving through.
- **Winter** — empty and free. Dead standing reed, water flat pewter, the sun
  behind the land. The quiet season is the one with no sunset on the water.

## Notes / cautions

- **No confirmed jetty.** Sources mention only "rock piles," possibly natural
  glacial cobble. Author freely, but do not assume a groin field exists.
- Marsh plant specifics are general Sound salt-marsh ecology, not a survey of
  this exact marsh. Fine for fiction; do not treat as verified fact.
