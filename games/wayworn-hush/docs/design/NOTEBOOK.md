# Wayworn Hush — The Notebook (design direction)

> **Status:** built, first pass. Kept general on purpose — the shape, not the
> content.

## What it is

**A collection, not a log.** Each thought the pilgrim has is a *thing he came to
on his own* — produced by where he was, what he'd already seen, and who he'd
become. It is the closest this game has to a prize. The notebook is where those
live.

The register to aim at is a creature index in a collecting game — but only half of
one. Every entry is an entity with its own worth, not a line in a transcript. What
it does *not* borrow is the checklist (see "The gaps are felt, not shown").

## Why it isn't a log

A transcript answers "what happened." A collection answers **"what have I come to
understand?"** — which is this game's whole question. The difference is felt: a
log is skimmed once and abandoned; a collection is returned to and *wanted*.

This also gives the game its quiet ambition. There are no enemies and nothing to
beat; the thing to pursue is a fuller notebook.

## Dated pages, and the note in his own words

It reads as pages: a dateline, then the notes he wrote under it, in the order he
wrote them. **A note is labelled by what it says** — its own opening words — not
by its rarity. Rarity is what a thought is *worth*, not which one it is; a column
of "Uncommon / Uncommon / Rare" is unreadable and unlike anything a person writes.
Rarity rides in the ink instead: the faculty gives a note its hue, the rarity its
brightness — already the vocabulary the reading box uses.

**Time passes; telling it is earned.** The world's clock runs whether or not the
pilgrim can read it — an instrument that tells him the hour is a thing he finds,
not the thing that makes time real. So every note is recorded with the moment it
happened, always. What a watch buys is *legibility*: without one he still knows
which day of the walk he was on (days are countable by living through them), and
with one the hour comes too. The record never loses what he couldn't read at the
time.

Read back, a note appears on the *same page it was written on* — the reading box's
paper surface. A thought should be one thing whether it is landing or being looked
up years later.

## Each entry is an entity

A thought already carries far more than the notebook shows. It is derived at
load, never authored twice:

- **Rarity** — how hard it was to reach. Already the roll's difficulty band, and
  already has a shared word + colour vocabulary the readings use.
- **Worth** — what having it is actually worth, from what it opens and how
  central it is to the web of understanding.
- **Faculty** — which way of seeing produced it, with its own hue.
- **When** — the day it landed, if the pilgrim could tell the time.

All of that is computed and then discarded at the notebook's door. Surfacing it
is most of the work: **the data is already there.**

## The gaps are felt, not shown

A collection needs edges to be motivating — but this one does not draw them. The
notebook holds **only what he has actually thought**. No blank rows, no count of
what's missing, no denominator.

This is the same rule the rest of the game follows: a miss is content, and the
game never summarises for you. A list of labelled blanks would turn walking into
filling in a form — the pilgrim would be chasing *the game's* list instead of
noticing things. What's out there should be felt by being out in it, not read off
an index.

The register to borrow from a creature index is **each entry being a prize**, not
its checklist. Those separate cleanly, and only the first one belongs here.

## One collection, one place

Readings and thoughts were split across two surfaces (a "noticed" list and the
notebook) as an admitted temporary state — the same record shown twice. **That
split is gone.** The notebook is the one place the pilgrim's understanding lives;
the pause page is where carried things are, and this is a carried thing.

## Register

It should read as *his* notebook — the pages of someone walking alone who writes
things down. Not a database, not an inventory grid. The existing surfaces' quiet
vocabulary (soft text, no hard chrome) is the floor, not the ceiling.

## Open threads (decided when built)

- Whether entries can be sought deliberately, or only met.
- Sorting / filtering / search, once there are enough notes to need them. The
  dated pages are the *default* order, not the only one — but whatever else is
  offered, the notebook should still open as a notebook.
- What a full notebook means, if anything — and whether it's even reachable.
- Whether the collection is per-pilgrim (it is, today) or something an
  installation accumulates across pilgrims.
- Whether an entry should carry more of its worth than a reward figure.
- Whether the observations a thought came from belong in it, now that they have
  nowhere else to live.
