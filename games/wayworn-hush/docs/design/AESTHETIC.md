# Wayworn Hush — Aesthetic

> **Owns:** art direction, palette, music direction, environmental
> storytelling register. This doc is a reference sheet for future
> authoring, not a locked spec.
>
> **Status:** initial doc — intent + references.

## Overall register

**Melancholic-uplifting.** Not sad, not cheerful — the specific texture
of *quiet after long walking*. Wistful. Grateful. Tender.

Think: the moment at dusk when the day's tiredness is real and the
evening is beautiful and both are true at the same time. That specific
feeling as a game aesthetic.

## Visual references

### Direct pixel-art references

- **Chrono Trigger (1995)** — the gold standard for pastoral 16-bit
  landscapes. Overworld maps, biome variety, warm interior spaces,
  weather-affected lighting, sprite-based portraits.
- **EarthBound / Mother 2 (1994)** — town design (small, homely, weird),
  overworld feel, whimsical NPC portraits. Halloween Hack mod for tonal
  darker adjacent.
- **Mother 3 (2006)** — the closest single reference. Pastoral pixel
  landscapes, deep emotional register, weather, day/night, animal
  wildlife, sparse settlements. If Wayworn Hush leans anywhere, it
  leans here.
- **Pokemon Ruby / Sapphire / Emerald (2002-2004)** — route design,
  weather system UI (rain / hail / sandstorm / sunny), overworld
  freedom mid-game. NOT the saturation level (Ruby is too vibrant for
  Wayworn Hush's palette), but the STRUCTURE of routes-as-experience.
- **Golden Sun (2001-2003)** — biome variety, ambient effects,
  overworld sense of scale.
- **A Short Hike (2019)** — modern voxel/pixel adjacent; not our art
  style, but the CAMERA sensibility (look up at things, stop at
  vistas) is right.

### Non-pixel references

- **Studio Ghibli — My Neighbor Totoro, Only Yesterday, Whisper of
  the Heart, Kiki's Delivery Service** — pastoral melancholy,
  attention to small everyday things, landscape as character.
- **Mushishi (2005-2006 anime)** — misty landscapes, quiet supernatural,
  the protagonist's attention as the show's subject. Study the SHOTS
  Mushishi uses: wide, still, patient. Wayworn Hush's overworld should
  feel like walking through a Mushishi episode.
- **The Wind Rises (Ghibli, 2013)** — ambient period landscape.
- **Kino's Journey (anime)** — solitary traveler, episodic small
  encounters, philosophical inner life. Very close tonal reference.

## Palette

> **Open tension (unresolved — do not treat the muted direction below as
> settled).** There's active interest in studying and replicating Pokemon
> Emerald's actual palettes and shading, with some EarthBound warmth mixed in.
> Emerald/EarthBound run *brighter and warmer* than the "muted, low-saturation"
> direction stated below. Both are valid; they pull different ways. This is a
> deliberate later decision — whether melancholy comes from desaturated color,
> or from music/pacing/writing over a brighter Emerald-register palette, or from
> a muted default with warm Emerald beats reserved for affective moments. Left
> open on purpose. See [SCALE.md](SCALE.md) open questions.

- **Muted, low-saturation.** Not vibrant. Not Pokemon Ruby's tropical
  brightness. Closer to Mother 3's Chapter 1 farm environments, or
  Chrono Trigger's Zenan Bridge fog scene, or Kino's Journey's late-
  evening travels.
- **Warm greens, cool blues, ambient greys**, with warm yellow-orange
  reserved for firelight (camping, lanterns, hearths). The warm-cool
  contrast in single scenes is what carries the mood — warm point
  lights inside cold environments.
- **Region-specific palette shifts.** Forests lean green-brown;
  coastlines lean grey-blue-white; mountains lean stone-grey-blue;
  wetlands lean muted-teal-brown; high-plains lean straw-gold-blue.
- **Weather shifts palette temporarily.** Rain desaturates; snow
  cool-shifts; mist lightens contrast; late-evening warm-shifts.
- **Time-of-day shifts palette.** Dawn cool-warm gradient. Noon
  balanced. Dusk warm-pink. Night blue-black with visible stars.

## Music direction

- **Ambient, layered, region-specific.**
- **Sparse.** Long stretches of near-silence with just wind, water,
  bird, footstep, distant animal. The absence of music is composition
  too.
- **Composed by you + your friend.** This is the project's core purpose.
- **Reference tracks / composers:**
  - Yasunori Mitsuda (Chrono Trigger overworld themes — the moment
    "Peaceful Days" comes on)
  - Shogo Sakai (Mother 3 — especially the New Pork City ambient
    tracks, but the SOFTER register)
  - Nobuo Uematsu (FF6 overworld, especially "Terra's Theme")
  - Toby Fox (Undertale — combat theme variety, dodge-pattern-music
    integration)
  - Ryuichi Sakamoto (Merry Christmas, Mr. Lawrence; The Last Emperor —
    pastoral piano)
  - Ólafur Arnalds — modern ambient piano-strings
  - Toshifumi Hinata (Mushishi score) — SPECIFICALLY THIS. The Mushishi
    OST is the closest tonal fit.
- **Combat music** shifts to a distinct register when a turn-based
  encounter starts — same instrumentation family, more forward rhythm.
  Not "battle music explosion" like Pokemon; more "the ambient bed
  tightens with strategy."
- **Camping music** — a specific brief theme for the camp scene,
  possibly with vocals-adjacent-oohs, warm-firelight-in-audio.

## Environmental storytelling

- **The world tells the story.** No exposition dumps. No lore books.
  What the player sees is what they know.
- **Small details reward attention.** A rain-worn footpath. A cairn
  built by someone before. A carved tree. An animal path. A cooking-
  fire ring beside a river. A rope hanging from a branch. Each of
  these is a small story the player can notice.
- **NPCs speak in gentle sparse lines.** Not tutorial dumps, not
  faction politics. A shepherd's brief mention of the weather. A
  villager's mention of a lost sibling. An old traveler's few words
  of caution about a distant pass. Each NPC exists for one small
  human moment, then the player moves on.

## Inner monologue register

- **The protagonist is not a chatterbox.** Long stretches of silence.
  Then a thought. Then more silence.
- **The thoughts get deeper as the game progresses.** Early monologues
  are surface: "The rain is cold." "I miss home." Mid-game monologues
  observe: "I've been walking three weeks. My mother would be worried."
  Late-game monologues cohere: "I think I know what I've been looking
  for. It isn't a thing." Ending monologues carry the arc: the
  protagonist arrives at something and articulates it, briefly,
  without spelling it out.
- **The register is gentle, grateful, plainspoken.** No poetry-that-
  screams-poetry. No pretension. A person thinking, in his own head,
  the way a person thinks.

## UI style

- **Minimal.** Text boxes. Menus. Simple frames. No flashy transitions.
- **Reference:** Mother 3's UI (frame styles, font choices,
  understated menus). EarthBound's dialogue windows.
- **Pixel-perfect fonts** — bitmap style, readable, unadorned.
- **Menus are small and quiet.** Inventory takes a corner of the
  screen, not the whole screen.

## What NOT to do (aesthetic anti-patterns)

- No vibrant tropical Pokemon Ruby palette
- No cyber / neon / high-saturation
- No horror lighting (Wayworn Hush is not gothic; the light is
  gentle even when the world is quiet)
- No epic-fantasy pageantry
- No dialogue portraits with elaborate expressions — sprite-scale
  faces only
- No text scrolling with clickety-clackety sound effects on every
  syllable (Earthbound does this and it works there; Wayworn Hush's
  register is quieter — soft text scroll or instant text)
- No exposed HUD stats during exploration — only during combat/menus
- No urgent objective markers, waypoints, quest arrows
