# Wayworn Hush — The Opening & the Two Places

> **Owns:** how the game begins, the voice that begins it, and the shape of the
> world the walk happens in. First draft — the shapes are settled, the specifics
> deliberately are not.
>
> Read [THEME.md](THEME.md) first: the subject underneath is never stated. This
> doc describes an opening whose whole method is that it does not explain itself.

## tldr

The game opens on a **narrator who may or may not be the pilgrim** — first
person, past tense, warm and specific about a life, styled as its own channel.
It hands off to the beach with no cut and no announcement; the transition happens
in logic, not in image. The player should feel the wrongness before being able to
point at it.

The world is **two places, deep rather than wide**: an open **shore** where he
begins, and a small **woods** that keeps opening. He does not lose his
personality — only his biography.

---

## 1. The narrator **[shape locked, identity deliberately open]**

- **First person, past tense.** "All this led me to the beach that one day."
  The past tense is the tell, hidden in a grammatical mood rather than in
  content: the story being told is already over.
- **It may or may not be him.** The player will assume it is. It reads more
  composed than he ever sounds in-game. The discrepancy is never explained and
  never resolved. **Do not settle this in content** — the ambiguity is the
  device, not an unfinished decision.
- **Its own channel.** Styled distinctly from in-game thought (a different
  typographic register), so it is legible as a separate voice without a word of
  explanation.
- **It must be genuinely good company.** Warm, specific, funny — a person
  talking about his life and his people. If the opening is vague dreamlike
  imagery it is arty and empty and the handoff costs nothing. The player has to
  *like him* before the ground changes.
- **It does not recur during the walk.** One appearance at the opening, one at
  the end. In between, the player lives entirely in his actual thoughts — so the
  contrast at the end says what no dialogue could.

**Why a separate channel and not his in-game voice:** a retrospective, assured
register running alongside the confidence arc ([VOICE.md](VOICE.md)) would fight
it — he cannot sound composed in the prologue and hedging in hour one without the
seam showing. A distinct channel earns the assurance for free.

## 2. The handoff **[LOCKED]**

- **No waking-up scene.** No cut, no title card, no "and then," no moment the
  game admits a transition occurred.
- **The transition is at the level of logic, not image.** The narration keeps its
  own internal sense while the ground underneath it changes. Disorientation
  through continuity, not through spectacle.
- **Nothing is explained afterward.** No one tells him where he is, and he does
  not ask the player to solve it. There is nothing to reveal — only something to
  arrive at.

## 3. What he keeps and what he loses **[LOCKED]**

**Personality survives; biography does not.**

- **Kept:** how he talks to himself, his humor, his reflexes, his habits — the
  texture of a specific guy. He is a person from minute one, which is what makes
  him funny from minute one.
- **Lost:** the facts. Name, house, the particulars of a life. Crucially, he
  cannot say **why he left** — the urgency is gone but the walking is not
  ([ORIGINAL_NOTES.md](ORIGINAL_NOTES.md): "maybe he doesn't know what he leaves
  home for").

What he recovers over the walk is **not information about his life**. It is
comfort in being himself — the confidence arc ([VOICE.md](VOICE.md)). The game
never trades in revelation.

## 4. The two places **[shape locked, extent open]**

**Deep rather than wide.** Two realised areas beat eight thin ones; the bet is
encounter density and revisit-value, which is exactly what the observation system
was built for.

### The shore — where he begins
Open, horizontal, exposed sky, weather visible coming from far off. You can see a
long way and nothing is hidden. It is the last place that is solid: mapped, has
stuff, has a horizon. A shore is a threshold and water is a crossing; he is on
the far side of something. Never stated.

### The woods — the place that keeps opening
Small, enclosed, short sightlines, mundane on its face. **Not endless in space —
endless in depth.** A bounded place authored to keep having more in it.

- **Ordinary by design.** Trees, rocks, a stream, more trees. What is hidden is
  not treasure or lore — it is *him*. An unremarkable log is where he finds out
  something about himself.
- **Recognition is the mechanic.** Returning to the same spot and reading it
  differently *is* the arc. Change cannot be felt against ever-new scenery; only
  against something that stayed put. This is why the woods are small.
- **Two kinds of change, kept distinct:**
  - *He changed* — same place, deeper reading (tiers/faculties). Internal, common.
  - *It changed* — something is literally different now (flags). External, faintly
    uncanny, and **rare**: two or three times across the whole game. Frequent
    visible shifting reads as a gimmick; rare shifting reads as *did that always
    look like that?*

**Why the woods need not be procedural or infinite:** novelty is not the goal.
Recognition is. A small place revisited does the theme's work; an endless one
cannot.

**Both places are drawn from real ones the author knows.** That is the point —
the observations get to be specific and true rather than generic wilderness.

## 4b. The opening carries the weights **[direction set, content open]**

There is no character creator and no origin picker. The naming screen names —
nothing else (declining yields the default name, per the fixed-protagonist
model). Who he starts as is expressed **inside the opening's ordinary play**:

- The first beats' choices — what he says, what he takes — are ordinary deeds
  that may carry **small stat weights**, like any deed after them. The player's
  first minutes are simultaneously characterization and the initial spread;
  there is no gate, no menu, and nothing waits on anything.
- Weights in the opening are **expression, not optimization**: small numbers, no
  wrong answers, every road playable. The first conversation must never read as
  a build screen.
- Future UI note: a small faculty glyph in its hue beside weighted choices
  (learning what the faculties ARE by seeing what feeds them). Quiet — a tint
  and a glyph, never a reward toast.
- The possible pre-walk questionnaire (see GAME-SYSTEMS §1, pre-lit core) sits
  beside the naming screen if it happens at all; it asks about the player, not
  about him.

## 5. Open questions

- **What draws him back into the woods?** Recognition needs a reason to return —
  does the game send him, or does he go because he suspects there is more?
- **The extent of the world.** Whether these two carry the whole walk or are the
  first two of more. Scope decision, not yet made.
- **What the narrator says at the end.** Only that it returns once is settled.
