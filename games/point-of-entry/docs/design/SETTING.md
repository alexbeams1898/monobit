# Point of Entry — setting & structure notes

> **Status: IDEAS ONLY. Nothing in this file is set in stone.** These are
> working notes from design conversation, captured so they don't evaporate —
> every entry is revisable, and entries marked OPEN are actively unresolved.
> General shape only; names, copy, and dialog come later.

## The town

- Upstate New York, Hudson Valley. A **village** wrapped in thick wood — not
  near a city; the wood is the edge of the world.
- **Small and working class.** Pokemon-town scale, on the order of twenty
  residents. Isolation is the point.
- Look: an Earthbound town. Reference: Brewster NY, but much smaller.
- In it: a **gas station / deli / convenience store** (consumables, the
  grocery layer — reference: Channingville Deli, Wappingers Falls), a
  **Lowes-like hardware box** (weapons, equipment, unlockables — THE gear hub
  until a later unlock lets him modify equipment himself), and **the bar**.

## The bar — one building, the whole life [feels right]

The game stacks vertically in a single landmark building:

- **His apartment upstairs.** Fresh out of trade school, one-man freelance
  business, no way he owns property — a room over the local is exactly his
  means. The cat lives here (see below).
- **The bar at street level.** His drinking buddies are the hallway, not a
  destination — every trip home passes the regulars, which makes the bar the
  town's mouth: ambient rumor about the quarantine and the strangeness drips
  nightly with no scenes.
- **The basement below — and the job.** The bartender/landlord learns his new
  tenant does pest control: ants have been coming up through the basement.
  Possibly *why the rent was cheap* — the deadpan joke and the working-class
  logic in one line.
- The first point of entry is in that basement. The coziest room in the game
  and the wound share plumbing; the regulars drink on top of the descent the
  whole game.

## The descent's shape — a delta converging on one root

- **One root at the bottom; its infestation branches upward** through veins.
  Every hole on a floor is a different trail from the same system — the
  trade's real method (follow the trail to the source; killing foragers is
  nothing) is the game's structure.
- **A hole is a SEED, not a door.** Space does not exist until the gadget maps
  it (already settled doctrine — this makes lazy generation diegetic). Digging
  a hole generates its floor once, permanently. Sealing the holes you don't
  follow is exclusion work — the player doing his job, not the game closing
  doors. Sealed is sealed until NG+.
- **Holes advertise.** Seeps are typed; the hole's kind — its art, what
  trickles out, what you can hear — is the preview. Picking a path is reading
  the infestation. Special floors (gifts, bosses) are special-looking holes.
- **Depth composes; the vein seasons.** The floor number carries the authored
  structure: species bands as acts, boss floors as act boundaries, shaped
  beats around them (breather before, gift after). Entering a kind of hole
  only *boosts* that kind on the next floor — one floor of decaying memory,
  and every floor guarantees at least one off-theme hole, so lock-in is
  impossible by construction. The Law of Depth stays the strongest axis:
  species evolve out from under their own theme as you go down.
- **The "root smell."** Proximity to the source is something he can smell —
  the Law of Depth made diegetic. Descending is following a scent gradient to
  the nest.

## Home, staging areas, and the reason to go back

- **Home is the main hub; staging areas are mini-hubs.** Over the game,
  *capabilities migrate* to staging areas (brew fills, spend points, later
  maybe equipment work) — diegetically, he outfits the site: a camp stove, a
  proper kit case, possibly rank-gated (a certified applicator runs a bigger
  field setup).
- **Collection never migrates.** Mail doesn't forward: checks arrive in the
  mailbox (a freelancer is paid by mail), the cat leaves gifts on the
  doorstep, fills live in his own cupboard, the bed is the bed. Home keeps
  its gravity to the very end because good things *accumulate* there.
- **Cozy-wrapped maintenance, always.** Every punishing or maintenance-shaped
  thing gets an Earthbound-style warm wrapper (the dad-phone-call / ATM
  principle). The pull home is warmth, not obligation.

## The bed — one scene, three uses

- **Game start, death, and NG+ are all the same shot: waking in his bed.**
- Death as a bad day at work that ends in your own bed, cat on the blanket.
  He never acknowledges nearly dying. The game never explains how he got
  home — comfort the first time, quietly the scariest thing in the game by
  the tenth, at zero scene cost.
- Settled in v1: death IS the ride home -- fade to black, wake at the
  apartment, whole, no asking screen. The dungeon floor and its progress are
  simply gone. (The literal bed shot arrives with the apartment's art.)

## The cat

- **An orange cat, living at the apartment**, later unlockable to bring on
  jobs. Passive attacker, upgradeable, equippable — very passive (the Mother
  dog / DQ-FF animal register).
- Passes the mechanic doctrine hard: **a mouser is the oldest tool in the
  trade.** Not a mascot — equipment. He treats it as a colleague; it has
  verified kills.
- Mechanically leaning: **a tool in the kit system scaling off Biological**
  (the organism-tool axis), not a bespoke companion system. Its "weapons" are
  its equipment slots.
- Later horror lever, if wanted: animals notice wrong things. A cat that
  won't come below a certain depth says more than a scene.

## The warning [replaces the earlier caller-twist idea]

- **The thing at the root tries to scare him off.** A beat at the first
  descent into the basement: a call or sign — ominous and wrong in the
  context of a normal extermination job — telling him not to proceed.
- He registers it as a prank call and goes back to work. The player gets the
  horror at full strength; he doesn't get it at all — the game's tone in one
  moment, placed where the loop begins.
- The unstated implication, never explained: something at the bottom of the
  world felt him start down the stairs and *flinched*. It doesn't want to eat
  him; it wants him to stop. Retroactively reframed by "best exterminator of
  all time."
- Leaning: **recurring at act boundaries**, the signs getting less like phone
  calls as depth grows — escalating attempts to scare a man who cannot be
  scared, running parallel to the bestiary ladder. His non-reaction is the
  punchline every time.
- Constraint held from the pitch: never named, never shown plainly, never
  explained. A horror beat, not lore.

## The quarantine

- Police **soft-quarantine** with a corrupt flavor: "we know something is
  going on and it could harm you — you still can't leave."
- OPEN: whether anomalies are ever *seen* around town, or the quarantine is
  the town's one visible wrongness.

## How it presents — and how it markets

- The game should appear, inside and out, as **"exterminator game"**: a
  normal trade sim-flavored action RPG. The storefront is in-register —
  trailer and screenshots show ants, invoices, a thermos; nothing below the
  early depths ever appears. The horror is discovered, not advertised, and
  the discovery is the word of mouth (the Doki Doki / Inscryption launch
  shape). The discipline is total: no tag, no screenshot, no dev post winks.

## Creature stats & THE SMELL [settled in shape, 2026-08-11]

> Named **smell**, not rot: rot names a process (a small theory); smell names
> only the EVIDENCE, which is all anyone has. Comedic, gross, simple -- the
> register. It unifies with the root-smell doctrine: stronger with depth AND
> stronger in hot individuals, one continuous fact. (Read "rot" below as
> "smell"; the working notes predate the rename.)

**THE SHEET [settled]: resistance / defensiveness / dispersal** -- all real
trade terms (resistance = survives treatment -> hp; defensiveness = the
stinging-insect term -> contact; dispersal = movement through a structure ->
speed). Souls-style cross-feeds: contact also reads body-weight (resistance
minor), speed also reads temper (defensiveness minor). xp derives from total
points. Depth adds points through a PER-SPECIES growth spread (ants gain
dispersal, mice gain resistance -- species deepen along their own character).
Smell multiplies all derived outputs. Reserved system names: "pressure"
(infestation pressure = the wave programs), "fecundity" (per-species spawn
output, if wanted).

- Creatures get a tiny SHEET + base power per species; hp/contact/speed are
  DERIVED (same derive-at-load doctrine as the player). Depth adds sheet
  points -- replacing the juice multipliers with one system. Sheet stat
  names still open; reference: prison-escape's enemy formulas.
- **ROT**: a per-individual roll (EV-like -- boosts all stats), replacing
  prison's "essence". Named rot BECAUSE the game never explains what the rot
  is -- the mechanic knows exactly as much as the man does. Roll range runs
  hotter with depth; high-rot individuals are worth more (xp, purer drops).
- **Evolution = enough rot.** `evolves.at_rot` replaces at_depth: the rot
  transforms bodies, depth governs statistically, and boundary depths get
  GRADIENTS -- the first evolved form arrives as a surprise among normals.
- Future scene (not actionable yet): deep floors leak visible rot pools; a
  creature runs to one, FEEDS (its rot number rises live), and evolves
  mid-fight if not killed first -- pure emergent consequence of rot being
  one live number everything derives from. Wants overworld animations.

## The Field Guide [settled in shape, 2026-08-11]

- Called THE FIELD GUIDE -- the trade's real object (the tabbed truck binder,
  ruler printed on the tab). Entry format mirrors the real thing: Description
  (size, marks) / Similar Groups (mis-ID risk) / Biology / Habits &
  Harborage / SIGNS (frass, smear marks, gnaw marks, footprints -- the
  evidence found BEFORE the animal) / Control. Tabbed by pest group.
- **The printed guide only knows what the trade knows.** Surface species have
  full entries from page one. The deep things HAVE NO ENTRY -- the book just
  ends where they should be. The absence is the horror beat; costs nothing.
- **He writes the missing entries himself**: penciled pages accruing from his
  kills and found traces -- measurements, signs, what worked. The
  best-exterminator motivation as a surface: not reading the record, WRITING
  it. Rank's verified kills are the same data wearing paper.
- Signs both directions: known species -- match a trace in the world to the
  printed page; unknown -- the trace lands in his notes first, unexplained
  until he meets the thing. No future-knowledge anywhere.
- **Kills are the key [settled]**: killing is the game's central verb, so
  per-species KILL TALLIES are the one substrate driving enemy progression --
  guide entries deepen at kill-count thresholds (his penciled notes grow as
  the record grows), and achievements/unlocks concerned with enemies read the
  same tallies. Rank's verified kills, the guide, and achievements are ONE
  ledger wearing three surfaces -- never three counters.
