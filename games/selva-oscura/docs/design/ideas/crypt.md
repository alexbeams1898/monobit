# Crypt + soul-pipeline ideas (not canon)

Working notes for the entrance-to-Hell structure on the colle's plateau,
plus the cosmological mechanics it implies (judgment, soul-pipeline,
circle states, second-death resets). Nothing here is locked yet — these
are concepts to mix/match before committing to canon in
[setting.md](../setting.md), [wood.md](../wood.md), and
[fallback.md](../fallback.md).

## A. The crypt — exterior

**Vitaleta x Rucellai hybrid.** Starts from
[Cappella della Madonna di Vitaleta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappella_della_Madonna_di_Vitaleta)
silhouette + [Rucellai Sepulchre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rucellai_Sepulchre)
wall skin.

### Keep from Vitaleta (silhouette)
- Single-cell rectangular nave, ~2:1 long:wide
- Pitched gable roof in terracotta (NOT marble — would read Roman triumphalism)
- Plain triangular pediment, empty tympanum
- Bell-arch (*campanile a vela*) rising from rear gable, single bronze bell
- Cypresses flanking the façade

### Replace with Rucellai (wall skin)
- Two-tone inlay: white travertine field + green serpentine (*verde di Prato*) geometric inlays
- Fluted Corinthian pilasters at corners + dividing each long wall into 3 bays, short walls into 2 bays
- Inlay panel band in the middle third of the wall — plain ashlar base course below, plain ashlar frieze above
- Reduced panel density vs Rucellai's 30: ~10 panels total, denser on façade than flanks
- Motifs: wheels, rosettes, lozenges, 8-pointed stars, laurel/oak wreaths, interlace knots. Geometric only, no figures
- One central façade panel as emblem — stylized wind-sail quoting Rucellai's *Fortuna* device (Fortune blowing the pilgrim to the threshold)

### Cornice/crown
- Replace Vitaleta's plain string-course with lily-crenellated parapet (*merlatura gigliata*) along the long flanks
- Single inscription line in Roman lapidary capitals — Dantean, e.g. *"Per me si va ne la città dolente"*. Weathered, half-worn

### Door
- Albertian classical architrave — flat marble jambs, plain entablature
- Heavy bronze or weathered oak with iron bands
- Low and narrow — pilgrim-stoop scale
- Empty tympanum above the door

### Omit
- The Rucellai lantern. A round Albertian lantern on a Vitaleta pitched roof reads as a folly. Bell-arch is the vertical accent.

### Weather everything
Lichen on the green inlays, lily merlons missing tips, inscription half-worn. The fiction: Albertian skin was added centuries ago to an older rural chapel.

### In-fiction implication
Once a poor rural chapel, then someone wealthy and learned commissioned the Albertian re-skinning as a threshold object. Tuscan piety + learned cosmology meeting at the gate of Hell. The "someone" is almost certainly Beatrice (see Section C).

### Anti-drift checklist
- NOT Baroque (no scrolls, swags, broken pediments)
- NOT a Roman temple (no portico, no free-standing columns, no metopes)
- NOT the Holy Sepulchre 1:1 (no Christian Anastasis iconography)
- NOT polychrome (two stones only + dark accents — three+ stones reads Cosmati/Venetian)
- NOT figural (no saints, no Madonna over door — geometry IS the theology)
- NOT a tower (low horizontal, one slim bell-arch accent)
- NOT pristine

### Proportions (initial defaults)
- Body: ~5m wide × 7.5m long × 5.5m tall (to pediment apex), 6.8m with bell-arch
- Façade width : height = 1 : 1.1
- Door height : façade height = 0.42
- Pediment height : façade height = 0.22
- Oculus diameter : façade width = 0.13
- Oculus center vertical position: 72% up the façade
- Bell-arch width : façade width = 0.38
- Roof pitch = 25°

## B. The crypt — interior

Reference image filed in conversation 2026-05-21: Doré-adjacent Dante-and-Virgil
painting showing the interior of a tall nave — vaulted/pointed ceilings far
above human scale, two saint statues in deep niches flanking the entrance,
hanging votive lamps, a vaulted apse-like recess at the far end, candles
lining a deep stair descending into a red glow at the bottom.

### Mood the image carries
- Vertical drama — ceiling far above human scale; player feels small
- Stone-and-shadow palette — dark warm browns, no color except the red of distant fire and candle flame
- Sanctified-then-corrupted register — saint niches still occupied, but the place leads down into something visibly hellish
- Candle and lamp light only — no daylight inside (or one shaft of pale light from a high window, as in the painting)
- Compression-then-release: low/narrow door (pilgrim stoop), then ceiling opens dramatically tall

### Architectural elements to study from the image
- Tall pointed/lancet arches over the central passage
- Apse-like vault at the far end framing the descent
- Deep wall niches with saint statues (life-size or larger)
- Hanging chains with oil lamps
- High narrow window admitting one pale shaft of light
- Coffered or vaulted ceiling
- Stone floor, large flagstones

### Tension with the exterior — impossible space
The outside is a ~5m-wide Vitaleta box; the inside is tall, narrow, vaulted,
deep. The interior CANNOT geometrically fit inside the exterior at face value.
Two options:

1. **Diegetic — the inside is impossibly larger than the outside.** Honest
   Dantean cosmology: thresholds to Hell don't respect Euclidean geometry.
   The player enters a small door and the world opens. **Leading hypothesis.**
2. **The body is just an antechamber; the descent itself is underground.**
   Inside the box is a small antechamber with the stair descending into the
   colle's interior — the tall vaulted space is technically carved into the
   hill below. Simpler engineering; less narrative bite.

### Saint statues — candidates
- Beatrice (anachronistic as a "saint" but iconographically central)
- Virgil (Limbo's most famous resident — guide-figure)
- Other unjudged souls? Or canonical figures (St. John the Baptist, the Annunciate Virgin)?
- Could be left ambiguous — saints whose attributes are eroded/unidentifiable, contributing to the corrupted-sanctified register

### The fire at the bottom of the stair
Strong visual; the player should NOT see this on first descent (the Acheron
fade-to-black happens before they reach the bottom). It's foreshadowing for
later runs / lore reveal. Or — the red light is Hell leaking through, visible
from the antechamber even before descent. Dantean: Hell announces itself.

## C. Crypt cosmology — Vagrant-only visibility

**Leading hypothesis: the crypt is visible to the Vagrant alone.**

Other unjudged souls in the Wood (and any other observer) see only trees
and the rise of the colle. They climb to the plateau and find *nothing built
there*. The Vagrant climbs, sees the chapel, descends through it.

### Why this works
- **Direct existential horror.** A building only you can see, on a hilltop, that leads down. Classic horror — but here it's destined, not malevolent. Worse.
- **Confirms the Vagrant's specialness through architecture.** He doesn't know Beatrice routed him here. He learns through chapel-visibility that something has been arranged. The Wood becomes uncanny — every NPC moves through a slightly different world.
- **Makes Beatrice's reach legible without showing her.** The chapel is her *fingerprint* on geometry. She has reached into the world and made a building visible to one soul and one soul only. That kind of authority lets the player imagine her without rendering her in-game.
- **Sharpens the Albertian inlay's purpose to a knifepoint.** The geometric panels are addressed to an audience of one. The patron commissioned it for the eyes that would see it, knowing the audience was him alone. One immortal building a thing for one mortal.
- **Justifies the over-elaboration cosmologically.** A normal rural chapel wouldn't have *verde di Prato* inlay. This one does because nobody but the Vagrant CAN see it — the strangeness was the first clue.
- **Justifies the weathering register.** Centuries with no caretakers; lichen and weather have been the only visitors.

### Mechanics required
- Other unjudged NPCs (if any) in the Wood must not navigate to or reference the chapel. Engine: simple visibility flag; NPCs path through it.
- Dialogue: at least one NPC denies the chapel exists when the Vagrant asks. *"You climb the hill and find nothing, vagrant. There is only the rise and the sky."* That's where the horror is delivered.
- First-sighting beat: the first time the Vagrant rounds the spine direction and the colle clears enough to see the chapel — designed moment (held camera, score chord, or austere silence).

### Dark variant (defer)
The chapel only APPEARS once the Vagrant has reached a certain state.
Pre-trigger: empty plateau. Post-trigger: chapel manifests. Stronger
narratively (revelation, threshold-crossing) but more engineering scope
(transition tech, before/after terrains). Hold as late-game cosmology beat,
not first-run mechanic.

## D. Soul-pipeline cosmology — Option A with refinements

**Leading hypothesis: judgment + imprint happen simultaneously at death,
off-stage, by divine act. Souls arrive at Acheron pre-imprinted.**

### The pipeline (in working Hell)
1. Death somewhere on Earth.
2. Divine judgment fires at the moment of death — measures the soul, writes the imprint, sorts to circle (or to Heaven/Purgatory). One act, instantaneous, opaque.
3. Damned soul appears at Acheron's near shore, already imprinted.
4. Charon ferries across.
5. Minos confirms placement (sorter, not measurer — Dante's tail-winding becomes routing, not judging).
6. Soul drops into its circle, begins contrapasso.

### Why Option A is clean
- **One judgment event, not two.** Cosmology simpler to hold; story simpler to write.
- **Divine judgment is opaque and instantaneous** — matches the *Commedia*'s register. Dante never shows the moment of damnation; that mystery is theologically appropriate.
- **Pushes cosmological action upstream.** The interesting parts of Selva's story are downstream (contrapasso, stagnation, riversamento). Judgment itself is just the supply source.
- **Minos demotes to logistical role.** Customs officer, not magistrate. Stays canonical without becoming a major cosmological mover.

### The unjudged — a natural leak in divine judgment

Per existing canon (setting.md ~137): *"Most unjudged souls do not end up in Hell. They go to the selva oscura — the natural destination of the unmeasured."*

So the unjudged-as-category PREDATES Beatrice. Divine judgment isn't omniscient/omnipotent — some souls slip past measurement. The cosmos has a fallback (the selvas) that catches them.

Possible flavors of how souls become unjudged (atmosphere only, not pinned):
- Die at a **threshold moment** — mid-sin, mid-repentance, mid-baptism, mid-confession — where the judgment mechanism can't resolve them
- Die in a **place outside the cosmos's normal regard** — far frontier, deep wilderness, the sea, places "God's eye does not fall"
- Death is **interrupted** — measured halfway, didn't complete
- Soul **refuses measurement** (the Guide's canonical case, setting.md ~607)

Mixing these gives the unjudged souls across the various selvas different backstories.

### Beatrice's actual role (clarified)

- She does NOT create unjudged souls — that's a natural cosmological category.
- She does NOT control the selvas — they predate her, they're natural cosmological features.
- She CAN reach into a selva and influence what happens there (she brought the light to the Vagrant's Wood — setting.md ~496).
- She CHOSE one specific unjudged soul (the Vagrant) and PLACED him at Hell's gates with intent. The "placement" is the transgression.
- She OPENED the crypt-gate that did not exist for any unjudged soul before. The crypt's existence — visible only to him — is her second transgression.
- She CANNOT make him damned. Unjudged status is a property of the soul, not a status she can change. Hell still can't grip him.

### The Vagrant's specialness is dual
- He's an unjudged soul (a natural cosmological category; others of his kind in other selvas).
- He's the unjudged soul Beatrice CHOSE and INSERTED into Hell (which makes him unique).

The first makes him eligible. The second makes him present.

### The crypt fits perfectly
- Damned souls never see or use the crypt (they don't arrive on Earth's surface — they appear at Acheron directly).
- Other unjudged can't see the crypt (Beatrice has gated visibility).
- The Vagrant alone sees it, uses it, descends.
- The crypt is truly outside the system — Beatrice's architectural improvisation inserted into a cosmology that wasn't designed to accept a living mortal through any door.

### Multiplayer implication (future possibility, do not design around now)
"Other unjudged souls in other selvas" maps cleanly to "other players in their own world-instances." Either:
- Each player is a Beatrice-chosen Vagrant in their own selva, meeting occasionally in shared Hell-space (Souls-style invasions/summons). Beatrice's transgression scales: she's chosen multiple unjudged.
- Or — co-op only inside Hell, never in the Woods. Woods stay private; Hell is the shared space.

Don't paint into a corner that forbids this later.

## E. Circle states — chaos vs enforced

**Leading hypothesis: each circle has two states, gated by keeper-defeat.**

### State 1 — Chaos (corrupt-keeper / unenforced)
- The corrupt keeper at the seat (transformed original or replacement
  parasite) is doing fuckall about the ring's law and a great deal of
  its own degenerate evil across the population
- Souls of the ring, who arrived to be punished according to their sin,
  instead encounter disorder shaped by the specific failure of this
  specific lost role (the Acheron pile + queue is the worked template:
  no Charon → souls don't cross → accumulate → dissolve → re-coalesce)
- Hand-placed encounters expressing the ring's specific chaos register
- Vagrant moves through methodically, resolves each via riversamento
- Keeper is the final encounter; killing them vacates the seat
- This is the "rampant Hell" the player walks into

### State 2 — Enforced (post-keeper-defeat)
- The vacated enforcement role re-installs into Beatrice's prepared
  NPC, mangling them into a wounded approximation of a proper keeper
- Under that new keeper, the ring's law is finally being enforced
  again — wind blows on the lustful, Cerberus rains on the gluttons,
  the river receives the violent
- Souls who had been caught in the chaos now encounter the actual
  punishment they were sentenced to
- Hostile encounters become procedural / dynamic / respawn-based
- This is what the circle SHOULD look like with a proper administrator
  (but scarred — never the same as divine-era Hell, per
  setting.md "Hell can be RESTORED but never PERFECTED")

### Why this works
- **Unifies cosmology with gameplay loop.** Pre-keeper = story-meaningful finite encounters in the ring's chaotic register; post-keeper = systemically-spawning respawn under restored enforcement. Same locations mean different things at different times.
- **Rewards the player's act of resolution.** Defeating the keeper restores enforcement to the ring. The player can FEEL the circle is functioning now — terrible in a different way, lawful instead of arbitrary.
- **Makes restoration a real cosmological act.** Vagrant's killing of the corrupt keeper enables Beatrice to install the new mangled keeper, which enables the law to be enforced again.
- **Explains "why now" for each circle.** The corrupt keeper could have been killed any time but only an unjudged soul can do it (Hell's accounting won't accept a damned killer). The Vagrant is the only mechanism.
- **Organic difficulty curve.** Pre-keeper bespoke and learnable (chaos register); post-keeper systemic and scaling (lawful register). Cleared circles become return-to resource zones.
- **Explains keepers cosmologically.** Corrupt keepers aren't just bosses — they ARE what's preventing the law from being enforced. Killing one vacates the seat for proper enforcement to resume.

### The central paradox
The Vagrant's act of resolving souls (riversamento) — which is PURITY's path — restores each ring's enforcement. He's inadvertently making Hell work again by trying to end it. PURITY's terminus (ending Hell) and the per-circle restoration are entangled. Late-game tension: he may not realize until very late that his progress has been restoring rather than destroying. *Extraordinary* narrative beat.

### Aesthetic implications
- Pre-keeper: the ring's chaos register, distinctive per circle. Not silence — the noise of a ring without its rule. Souls caught in arbitrary disorder thematic to the specific lost role (Acheron template: pile of dissolved bodies, queue facing water-that-isn't-coming).
- Post-keeper: hum, motion, fresh blood, screaming — but ORDERED. The sounds of a circle that is *processing*. Different ambient audio, lighting tier, particle work. Player walks back into a functioning ring and immediately FEELS the law is running again.

### Boss arenas post-defeat
After the keeper falls, the arena becomes a normal area of the circle —
possibly the location where the resumed circle's "fresh souls arrive"
mechanic happens. Defeating the keeper turns their throne-room into the new
pipeline entry.

### Keeper-defeat presentation
Souls does this with "GREAT ENEMY FELLED." Selva needs its own ritual.
The keeper's death triggers a longer beat — circle visibly thawing, fresh
souls arriving, audio shifting from frozen-silence to working-Hell ambience.
Make the player FEEL the cosmological state change.

## F. Second death + circle reset rule (proposed canon addition)

**Existing canon recap** (setting.md ~75, fallback.md ~89):
- Second death = Hell's killing-protocol fires on the Vagrant, his body dies, soul ejects to the Wood
- Cycle restarts because Hell cannot complete a protocol on a soul it never measured
- **Persists across cycles:** lifetime sangue, stats, riversato total, class, keepers felled, NPC encounter history, Grimoire unlocks
- **Resets each cycle:** wallet sangue, HP, position, transient combat state

### Proposed addition — the keeper as cosmological commit point

**The keeper IS the circle's stagnation lock.** While the keeper stands, the
circle is frozen — its static population is held in suspension. The
Vagrant's riversamentos on those souls pre-keeper are **provisional** —
held in a buffer that the keeper's lock prevents from committing.

When the Vagrant suffers second death in an unfinished circle, Hell's
killing-protocol firing on him also restores the locked circle to its
default static state — the same mechanism that resets his body resets the
circle's frozen population back to its initial snapshot. The buffer clears.

Defeating the keeper **breaks the lock** — the buffer commits, resolved
souls stay resolved across all future cycles, and the circle thaws into
its resumed state. The keeper-fall is the transactional boundary.

### Why this works cosmologically
- Single mechanism (Hell's killing-protocol) has two effects: reset Vagrant body, reset locked circle. Tight.
- Threads consistently with existing canon: "Hell cannot finish what it never began" — the same protocol that fails to finish the Vagrant successfully restores the locked-circle default.
- Maps cleanly onto Souls bonfire-commit design conventions.
- Gives second-death real bite beyond losing wallet sangue. Pre-keeper progress is at risk.

### Proposed canon edits
- **setting.md** *Cycle structure* (lines 193-200): add "in-progress damned-soul resolutions in any locked circle" to the reset list; add "resolved souls in any felled-keeper circle" to the persist list.
- **fallback.md** *Cycle persistence* (lines 89-95): mirror the same additions.

### Pending decision
Whether to formally write this as canon now or hold it as an idea. Recommend
write-as-canon because it's load-bearing for level design and the loop is
genuinely better with this rule than without.

## H. The larval damned — judged-but-unprocessed souls

**The third soul-state category** (alongside damned-in-circle and unjudged).
Souls who died, were judged by divine act, acquired their imprint, and were
delivered to Acheron's near shore — but who never crossed the river,
never reached Minos's confirmation, never descended to their circle, never
began their contrapasso. They are **judged but unformed**.

### What they are
- Sentenced to a specific circle, but never delivered to it.
- Have an imprint (judgment fired) but no started process (contrapasso never landed).
- Distinct from damned-in-circle (vivid, articulate, sculpted by centuries of contrapasso) and from unjudged (no imprint at all).
- Larval / pre-formed: their punishment never shaped them, so their identity is half-rendered.

### Visual / sensory register
- **Indistinct features.** Faces unmarked by the suffering that would have shaped them. Generic, undifferentiated.
- **Inarticulate.** No defined voice; moan, wail, fragments only. (In the *Commedia*, contrapasso gives the damned their voice — Francesca speaks of love because her windstorm shaped her speech. The larval damned have no contrapasso to give them a voice.)
- **Unrecognizable.** A learned visitor couldn't identify even a famous historical figure among them. The punishment hasn't marked them yet.
- **Partially-formed traces.** Some show early-stage marks of their assigned contrapasso that aborted when delivery jammed — bark beginning at fingertips that never spread, perpetual lean of being-blown that never moved, a glow of inner flame that never ignited. The shape of the sentence is visible in trace but not realized.
- **Bodies that look like Hell didn't finish drawing them.** Limbs in wrong proportions. Features sketched, not modeled.
- **Silent except for moaning.** Centuries of accumulated dread, no scream-drama (that's for Hell-proper). A low, continuous, collective moan.

### The mounds at Acheron's near shore
- Vast, dim, low ridges of indeterminate bodies receding into mist.
- Scale: hundreds of thousands or millions. Centuries of jammed delivery, the whole of post-medieval damnation, piled.
- The mounds shift slightly with the breath of shared lethargy.
- Visible to the Vagrant during his crossing of Acheron.
- **The scale itself is the horror.** Don't render individuals; render mass.

### Why the Vagrant cannot riversamento them
- Riversamento works on souls who refused completion of their contrapasso. Requires an imprint AND an unfinished process.
- The larval damned have an imprint but no started process. They are *pre-process*, not unfinished.
- Riversamento has nothing to complete because the contrapasso never began.

### Pathways to their resolution
- **Circle-resumption.** As the Vagrant restores each circle (via keeper-defeat), the larval damned bound for that circle finally descend, begin contrapasso, become themselves. Then — over time — they become riversamento-eligible.
- **Accelerated forming.** Possibly: the Vagrant's presence accelerates contrapasso. Or his act of seeing them triggers the long-frozen process. Open mechanic for late-game.
- **Untouched / unresolved.** Most likely outcome for the vast majority: the player's run ends, the mounds remain. PURITY may resolve them en masse as cosmic consequence; SURFEIT may erase them as Hell ends; REFUSAL leaves them stuck. The mounds are too large to clear individually.

### Beatrice's motivation, sharpened
She has seen TWO horrors for centuries: damned-in-circle in eternal frozen repetition, AND larval damned at the shore in indeterminate suspension. Both stagnation, both unbearable. Her snap is now grounded in two distinct horrors, not one.

### The Acheron near shore as cosmological tableau
This is a **Selva-original** that you can't find in the *Commedia* — but it reads as "Dante's Acheron, but broken." Where Dante saw Charon ferrying wailing damned, the Vagrant sees the same site with the process paused: formed-active-departure replaced by formless-silent-accumulation. Players who know the source will recognize the absence of what should be there.

## I. The Acheron traversal — full walked sequence (no fades)

Souls-style: every step is walked. No menu transitions. The descent IS
the cosmological transit; player feels the weight of it.

### Stage 1 — The crypt antechamber
- Low door admits the Vagrant.
- Inside: tall vaulted interior (per Section B reference image). Saint statues in niches flank the entrance. Candles, hanging oil lamps. One shaft of pale light from a high window.
- The interior is **impossibly larger than the exterior** (Section B Option 1).
- At the far end: vaulted apse-like recess frames the head of a deep staircase going down.
- **No danger.** Sanctuary-coded. Player can stop, look, absorb. The chapel is the threshold-protection.

### Stage 2 — The staircase descent
- Long, deep stair through cut stone. Several minutes of walking on first traversal.
- Walls transition: Tuscan-stone gives way to older, chthonic, less-identifiable masonry as the descent deepens.
- Light transitions: candles in the upper stair → gradual reddish glow from below or faint sangue-luminescence in walls as the descent progresses.
- **Tutorial-combat encounters here** (Section J). Sparse, paced — first encounter ~1/3 down, second ~2/3, third near the bottom.

### Stage 3 — Emergence onto Acheron's near shore
- Staircase ends. The Vagrant steps into a vast open space. Ceiling lost in darkness above. Ground extends out into mist.
- **The mounds visible.** Vast, dim, receding into mist. The collective moan. The horror lands as a single beat — held camera moment / no music / just the moan.
- Player can choose to walk toward the mounds (optional exploration) or along the shore toward the crossing.
- Sparse larval-damned encounters on the shore itself (per Section J). Optional; killing them is theologically inert (they regenerate to the mound).

### Stage 4 — The crossing
Working hypothesis: **Option B — congealed sangue wade.** Acheron's substance has thickened into a slow viscous black field. The Vagrant wades across.
- Wading is slow; substance resists. Possibly whispers or voices in the substance as he moves through.
- **Environmental hazard** — stamina drain and slow HP drain. The Vagrant must manage reserves to make it across. Souls-traversal-challenge register (Heide's Tower, Crown of the Old Iron King).
- The crossing takes minutes of slow movement. Far shore visible but slow to approach.
- The crossing is **purgatorial**. The player will remember it.

Alternative crossings if Option B is too punishing for transit:
- Option A: dry exposed river-bed, atmospheric not hazardous.
- Option C: a stone bridge that was always there (Beatrice's back-door).
- Option D: improvised stepping-stones / wreckage of Charon's last ferry.

### Stage 5 — Far shore + first steps into Limbo
- Empty far shore. No mounds — far shore is the side Hell processes from, processing is suspended.
- **One resonant detail:** the empty mooring post / iron ring set in stone where Charon's ferry was tied. Or: Minos's massive form, distant, motionless (he doesn't measure the Vagrant — no imprint to read, and Minos isn't currently working).
- First Limbo enemies appear at or near the far shore. Combat danger begins here.

### Transit cost
- First traversal: 15-25 minutes of real-time walking.
- Subsequent runs (working hypothesis): the descent (Stages 1-3) is skippable via a checkpoint at the staircase bottom. Stage 4 (the crossing) is walked every run as the cost-of-entry.

### Why no fades
- Player experiences every cosmological transit step.
- The crypt-and-stair is not just *flavor architecture* — it's the literal mechanism. The player understands their place in the world by walking it.
- This is the Souls bargain: no quick-travel mid-sequence; the world's geography earns weight by being traversed.

## J. The stair encounter — larval damned as tutorial

**The first encounter of the player's life.** Lore-coherent, mechanically
gentle, narratively load-bearing.

### What they are
The same larval damned that pile at Acheron's near shore (Section H), but
a handful have crawled up the staircase. Drawn — by the only thing for
centuries that has descended this stair. The Vagrant's presence summons
them, sluggishly.

### Why they make perfect tutorial enemies
- Slow, weak, no real attack. They reach, grasp, claw weakly.
- 2-3 hits to kill with the player's just-chosen class.
- Architecturally constrained stair geometry limits engagement complexity.
- Failure here costs nothing meaningful (Vagrant respawns in Wood; little progress lost).
- Player gets to use the class they just picked, in a forgiving context.

### Encounter pacing on the stair
- **~1/3 down:** one alone. The player's first fight. Pure tutorial.
- **~2/3 down:** two together. Mild pressure.
- **Near bottom:** three or four in a loose group. Last test before Stage 3 reveal.

### The retroactive horror payload
- The player kills the first larval damned, thinks it's a monster, moves on.
- They kill more as they descend. Build a mental model of "stair-creatures."
- They emerge onto Acheron's shore and see the mounds. **The same sound the stair-creatures made plays across the shore.** Audio-tied reveal.
- The player realizes: those weren't monsters. Those were *people*. Or what should have been people. **They have been killing pre-people without knowing.**
- This is deeply Dantean. Dante weeps for Francesca. The Vagrant has been killing larval Francescas in his first moments of awareness, retroactively.

### Visual / sensory specifics
- Read as **vague humanoid**, not monster. Not "rat from Fallout" clarity — these are vague human shapes. Player should fight them and not be entirely sure what they are.
- Death animation: not a clean ragdoll, not a monster scream. Something like **collapsing back toward mound-shape** — the soul un-forms, sinks. Previews what they are.
- Corpse: doesn't disappear like normal enemy corpses. Melts back into the floor, or coalesces into a small dark stain that fades over seconds. Already half un-formed; more so after death.
- Sound: the wailing/moaning is the same continuous moan-tone heard across the mound field at Acheron, here singular instead of choral.

### The theological-inert mechanic
- The Vagrant CAN kill them (Hell's killing-protocol fires on contact with the unformed imprint they carry).
- The Vagrant CANNOT riversamento them (no started process to complete).
- Killing them is **self-defense, not resolution.** No riversato granted. No cosmological progress.
- The "dead" larval damned likely **regenerates back to the mound** (returns to its un-rest in the pile).
- The player will not know this at first. Later, when riversamento is understood, the player realizes: the tutorial kills were *nothing accomplished*. The Vagrant has been swatting away pre-people without doing his job.
- Retroactively re-interpreted as "tutorial that did nothing."

### On the Acheron near shore (Stage 3)
- The same larval-damned can appear sparsely as encounters on the shore itself, not just in the distant mounds.
- They wander individually away from the mound toward the shore-edge.
- The Vagrant can fight them or ignore them — both are valid.
- All the same rules apply (killing is inert, they respawn to the mound).
