# Creatures (Selva-organisms)

> **Owns:** the ecology of the Wood — the organisms spawned from
> sangue surfacing into the Wood from Hell, their evolution, the
> player's capture and influence mechanics, the bestiary half they
> occupy.
> **Status:** lore and framework locked; specifics TBD.

## What these creatures are

The Wood contains a living **ecology** of organisms. They are
**not** Hell-creatures. They are **not** damned souls in animal form,
**not** hypertrophic damned, **not** minor demons. They are *new
life* — a biological response of the Selva to the novel environmental
input of sangue surfacing through the soil from Hell.

This distinction is **load-bearing.** Hell-creatures (keepers,
shades, the hypertrophic damned per [bestiary.md](bestiary.md)) are
*spirit-substance, post-mortem, judged.* They are punishment given
form.
**Selva-organisms are alive.** They have biology, lineages,
reproductive cycles, real evolution. They are what *life made* of
the sangue that surfaced.

This means the bestiary has two distinct halves:

- **Hell-bestiary** ([bestiary.md](bestiary.md)) — *figura umana* rule;
  damned souls and Hell's classical guardians; spirit-substance.
- **Selva-bestiary** (this doc) — *biological* organisms; novel life
  evolved from sangue that surfaced into the Wood; not derived from
  any damned soul.

The two halves are scientifically and cosmologically separate. A
shade in Hell is not a relative of any Selva-organism — they share
only that *both are made of sangue,* the way a stone wall and a
loaf of bread both contain carbon. Substance is shared; form is not.

## Cosmological framing

Hell is failing (per [setting.md](setting.md), per
[wood.md](wood.md)). Sangue surfaces out of Hell into the threshold-
Wood — both the baseline cumulative suffusion across centuries and
the per-circle surges that follow each keeper's death (see setting.md
*Per-circle reactivity*). Sangue is **inert by itself** — it is just
*stuff*. But the Wood is a biome with conditions (water, soil, light,
time-stop) that, until the suffusion began, supported only *vestigial*
life — moss, fungi, the simplest organisms — because nothing else
had reason to be here.

The suffusion introduces **novel conditions**: new soil chemistry,
new energy sources, new niches. The Wood's vestigial life **responds**
the way real life responds to new environmental inputs: through
genetic variation, selection, speciation, and evolution. New
organisms emerge that are *adapted to the new conditions.*

This is not magic. It is **medieval natural philosophy** —
specifically, the medieval theory of *spontaneous generation* (that
new conditions in matter produce new life) — and modern evolutionary
biology, sharing the same insight in different vocabulary. Dante's
audience would have accepted this framing unquestioningly: substance
plus conditions plus time produces life. Modern audiences accept
the same thing through Darwin. The mechanic spans both worldviews.

The Vagrant is therefore the **unintentional founder of a new
ecosystem.** By giving keepers second death — breaking the
contrapasso of their circles and letting the sangue of the unbound
damned surface — he is responsible for the existence of every
Selva-organism that ever spawns. They are, in a real sense, *a
coincidence of his work.* The unburdened path, especially, develops
a relationship with this — pouring sangue out is not just a
cosmological act; it is **planting.**

### Selva-organisms are NOT mission targets

This is load-bearing for the moral frame of the game.

The Vagrant's **mission** is in Hell: extracting substance, fighting
the keepers, forcing **second-death** upon damned souls and their
guardians. That is his sacred work, his cosmological role per
[setting.md](setting.md). The Hell-bestiary creatures
([bestiary.md](bestiary.md)) are *the targets of that work.* They owe
the cosmos repentance. The Vagrant grants it.

**Selva-organisms are not targets of any of this.** They are not
damned souls. They are not part of Hell's accounting. They owe the
cosmos nothing. They are **innocent life** that arose as a side-
effect of sangue surfacing — a coincidence of where the Vagrant
resides between descents. The Vagrant has *no cosmological obligation*
to them whatsoever — neither to harm nor to help.

This means the capture / bonding mechanic is **incidental to his
mission**, not part of it. Bonding with a Selva-organism is a
*choice he makes as a person*, not a duty he performs as the
unjudged. Two completely different relationships with two completely
different registers:

- **In Hell:** judgment, extraction, forced repentance, sacred duty.
- **In the Wood:** kinship, choice, gentle bonds, personal relation.

The same Vagrant inhabits both registers. He returns from Hell —
where he has just killed dozens of shades — to a Wood where a small
organism approaches him, and he may, by choice, name it. *That is
the game's emotional shape.* He extracts in Hell; he receives in
the Wood. The two are different verbs in different places, and one
of the player's discoveries is the contrast.

This also resolves a tension about whether the Vagrant *can* kill
Selva-organisms. Physically, of course — they are creatures, not
ghosts. But doing so is **incidentally cruel**, not mission-justified.
A Vagrant who kills indiscriminately in the Wood chooses cruelty
without cosmological purpose. The unburdened path probably never
does this; class-pickers may, occasionally, for materials or self-
defense. The mechanic carries moral texture without requiring an
explicit "morality system" — the player simply senses that killing
in the Wood is *different* from killing in Hell, because what they
are killing is *different*.

## The four interacting layers

The ecology emerges from **four interacting layers**, in this order
of weight:

### Layer 1: Sangue composition (linear, set by circles broken)

The Wood's soil holds sangue from each circle restored across the
descent, layered. The substance is always sangue; the *coloration*
(and the kind of organism it supports) is the signature of the
circle whose hemorrhaging damned surfaced that sangue in the
failing-state, plus the keeper-kill restoration surge. The composition
is a vector of how much circle-signature sangue is present.

- Pre-descent: pure **vestigial Wood substrate** plus the baseline
  centuries-cumulative sangue (per [wood.md](wood.md) *Lore — the
  Wood and Hell's failure*) that suffused before any keeper fell.
- After breaking Limbo (Circle I): vestigial + Limbo-signature
  sangue surfaces.
- After breaking Lust (Circle II): vestigial + Limbo + Lust-signature.
- ...and so on.

Circle-breaking is canonically linear (per Dante's architecture of
Hell), so the **order of sangue-signature addition is fixed.** This
is a constraint, not a freedom.

But the *composition itself* enables a possibility space of
organisms. Different compositions are different *biomes* — they
support different life. A Limbo-signature soil supports a certain
kind of organism; a Limbo+Lust soil supports different organisms
(often *hybrids*, exploiting niches that didn't exist with Limbo
alone); Limbo+Lust+Gluttony supports yet a third set. As the
composition grows more complex, the *possibility space* of organisms
expands combinatorially.

This produces the canonical Dantean *contrapasso* tone in biology:
a *Lust+Gluttony* hybrid is an organism that *consumes itself* —
because lust feeding gluttony is endless desire. The biology *reads*
as cosmologically legible. Not all hybrids will be this elegant; some
will just be ecological niche-fillers. But the *load-bearing ones*
will be canonically pointed.

### Layer 2: Player behavior (non-linear, set by the player's actions)

Substance is **inert** until conditions activate it. The catalyst
that turns substance into organism is the **player's behavior.**

Behavior categories that act as evolutionary pressures:

- **Combat style.** Aggressive vs defensive. The organisms in
  regions where the Vagrant has fought aggressively face frequent
  combat exposure; those that survive are *more combat-fit.* Same
  substance, different expression: aggression / armor / damage
  tolerance is selected for.
- **Economic path.** Sangue-installation (OFFERINGS, class-picker)
  vs sangue-pouring (riversamento, unburdened) produces different
  selective pressures on captured creatures. Installers produce
  *Hell-aligned* organisms (sangue-saturated, almost Hellish);
  pourers produce *Wood-aligned* organisms (more rooted, more
  plant-like).
- **Spatial behavior.** Where the Vagrant lingers, walks repeatedly,
  places cairns (when that verb ships) — the Wood notices. Local
  populations cluster around player-presence-sites.
- **Hell-side kills.** The substance of what the Vagrant has killed
  in Hell appears in the Wood. A Vagrant who has killed many gluttony-
  shades produces echo-substance in the Wood; the organisms there
  evolve to *exploit* it. Killing-history is heritable selection
  pressure.

This is **literal evolution.** The player IS the environment for
captured organisms (and partially for wild ones). Two players who
consume the same circles in the same order encounter **completely
different Woods** because their behavior differs.

### Layer 3: Spatial layering (non-linear, set by location)

The Wood is not uniformly saturated. Different zones have different
substance compositions:

- **Deep center of inner Wood:** pure vestigial-Wood substrate.
  *Oldest* organisms — the ones that were here before Hell began
  leaking. The most stable populations.
- **Spine direction (toward the colle):** sangue-saturated.
  *Highest* per-circle sangue concentration. The newest, most-Hell-
  marked organisms. Aggressive selection pressure favors organisms
  that can withstand or exploit dense sangue.
- **Specific outer-Wood spots:** unique micro-ecologies. A riversamento
  site (sangue poured pure) supports gentler organisms. A shade-grave
  (where the Vagrant once killed many shades) supports
  predator/scavenger forms.
- **Distance from inner Wood:** the further into the outer Wood, the
  *stranger* the niches and the *less* stable the populations. The
  far reaches are biological frontier.

Exploration is rewarded by *discovering* zones with different
ecologies. Different organisms cluster in different places.

### Layer 4: Temporal evolution (slow non-linear, set by cycles + time)

The ecology has its own dynamics independent of player action.
While the Vagrant is in Hell, the Wood's ecology continues to
**evolve.** When he returns, things have changed.

- Wild populations age, breed, die, drift.
- Predator/prey dynamics play out (some organisms eat others).
- Competition between species shifts the trait distributions of
  surviving populations.
- New variation arises through mutation in offspring.

This is the *between-cycle ecology tick.* The player returns from
each Hell-descent to a Wood that has *lived* in their absence. This
gives Wood-visits between descents a strong motivation — *what has
my ecology become while I was gone?*

(Lore reconciliation: the Wood is time-stopped at the *substrate*
level — deadfall doesn't rot, stones don't weather. But surfacing
sangue is *dynamic* — and the life it supports is dynamic too. The
Wood-substrate is static; the suffusing sangue and its life are not.
The static substrate is what makes the dynamic life *visible* — it
has a stage to perform on.)

## What the Wood remembers — persistence

The Wood is the only place in the cosmology where things accumulate
across cycles (per [wood.md](wood.md), per [setting.md](setting.md)
*Sangue and the law of substance*). For creatures, this means:

- **Wild ecology persists.** Populations, traits, locations, lineages
  — all saved between sessions, all carried across in-game cycles.
  The Wood remembers what lives in it.
- **Captured organisms persist.** Each captured organism is an
  individual instance saved per the Vagrant — its traits, its age,
  its evolution history, its lineage.
- **Sangue composition persists.** Once a circle has been broken and
  its signature sangue has surfaced into the Wood, it stays. Sangue
  that has surfaced does not leave the Wood.

This is the foundation that makes the entire mechanic work: **the
Wood is a real place with a real biology that the player is
*inside.*** Not a randomizer.

## Capture and bonding

The Vagrant can **capture** organisms — bond with them, take them
into his care, direct their further evolution by his behavior.

Capture is **not violent.** No traps, no projectile-balls. The
mechanic must respect both the Vagrant's register (solemn,
Dantean) and the creature's status (an organism, not a prize).
Working candidates:

- **Sangue-bond.** The Vagrant offers a small portion of his own
  sangue to the organism. If accepted, the organism recognizes him
  as kin — both made of sangue. Cosmologically clean.
- **Witnessing-bond.** The Vagrant spends time with the organism
  without disturbing it. After enough sustained attention, it
  follows him. Humble, patient, Beatrice-coded. Especially apt for
  the unburdened.
- **Naming-bond.** The Vagrant names the organism (player text
  input). Names are powerful in Dante's universe — to name is to
  hold in account. Once named, the organism is in the Vagrant's
  care.

**Likely synthesis:** the universal mechanism is **naming**, but the
*precondition* to be able to name an organism varies by species —
some need to be fought first, some need to be fed, some need to be
witnessed without threat. The mechanic for each species reflects its
*nature.* Specifics TBD.

## What captured organisms do

A captured organism's role in gameplay. *Multiple categories may
ship at different times — v1 is likely the smallest set.*

- **Passive presence in the Wood** — lives in your care, persists,
  visible when you return. The simplest category. *Likely v1.*
- **Crafting source** — produces materials over time under the
  Vagrant's care (shed scales, dropped feathers, secreted resin).
  Not killed for materials; given by living organisms. *Likely v1.*
- **Information source** — some organisms *sense* things. Point
  toward NPCs hidden in the Wood, react to upcoming events, indicate
  changes in the cosmology. *Living tools.* Probably mid-development.
- **Combat ally** — follows the Vagrant into Hell, fights alongside.
  Has its own stats, can die. (If killed in Hell — does Hell
  reclaim it? Cosmologically: yes, but the bond means part of its
  substance is the Vagrant's, and it may *re-form in the Wood* when
  the Vagrant respawns. Beautiful mechanic, complex implementation.)
  Later development.
- **Mount** — large enough to ride; fast traversal in the Wood,
  possibly through Hell. Big design layer. Late development.

The mechanic does *not* need to include all of these. V1 may be
"passive presence + crafting source" — captured organisms live in
the Wood, give materials over time. That is already deeply
satisfying. Other roles expand as the project matures.

## Bestiary entries — readable cosmology

The Selva-bestiary doubles as the player's **hint system** for the
ecology's logic. Each entry describes the organism in lore-rich
terms that reveal its substance composition and what it adapted
from:

> *Observed only where Limbo's substance has mingled with the long-
> standing damp of the deep Wood. A pale, sightless creature. It
> moves toward sustained sound, perhaps remembering something its
> substance once heard.*

The player learns over time to read these entries and back-derive the
cosmology — "ah, the Phlegethon-touched ones always have red
streaks; this one has them, so something near it is sangue-warm."
This is **reading cosmology through biology**, which is the
mechanic's highest expression.

## How the player learns the system

The mechanic is rich but it must not be didactic. Player learning:

- **First encounter is small and legible.** Early in the game, the
  first Selva-organism the player meets is *very obviously* tied to
  *something they just did.* They make the connection unaided.
- **Bestiary entries.** Lore-rich descriptions accumulate. Patterns
  emerge.
- **NPC dialogue references it obliquely.** *The Wood reflects the
  soul that walks it.* Hints, not exposition.
- **Comparison.** A player who watches another player play, or
  replays the game, sees their Woods are visibly different. This is
  the strongest possible legibility — they figure out themselves
  that *their behavior shaped this.*

The mechanic must never be explained in a popup. It is *discovered*
by play.

## The first organism the player meets

The player's *first* contact with the ecology should be a small,
memorable, **non-threatening** encounter — and should be *causally
clear.* Specifics TBD, but the shape:

- Small, slow, non-hostile organism.
- Appears in a specific spot the player has frequented (lingered
  near, walked past repeatedly, done a specific act near).
- Drops a single small material — *the Vagrant's first gathered
  Selva-substance.*
- Catalogs as the first bestiary entry. The entry text gives the
  player the first hint about substance composition.
- Establishes that the Wood *is alive*, *responds*, and *gives.*

This first moment is the load-bearing introduction to the entire
ecology mechanic. It deserves authorial care when it ships.

## Engineering — incremental architecture

The mechanic is real biology simulation. To ship it without going
all-at-once, an incremental architecture:

**V1 (smallest viable):**
- A handful of species defined (5–10), each with fixed stages.
- Static populations seeded at procgen time, persistent across
  cycles.
- Each organism has type + position + age + simple state.
- Encounters: when player enters a Wood zone, sample N organisms
  from local population to express visually.
- Capture: name-bonding for one or two species, no real evolution
  yet (captured organism is static).
- Bestiary entries: hand-authored for each species.

**V2 (selection emerges):**
- Each organism has a small **trait vector** (5–8 floats: size,
  aggression, sangue-tolerance, color, etc.).
- Traits are *heritable with variation* in reproduction.
- Environmental conditions apply selection: organisms with traits
  fit for their niche survive; mismatches die.
- Populations *drift* over real-time / cycle ticks. Visible to
  player: same species, different traits over months of play.
- Captured organisms evolve under the player's behavior-as-pressure.

**V3 (deeper biology):**
- Speciation (populations diverge into new species under sustained
  isolation).
- Niche differentiation (multiple species share a region by
  exploiting different resources).
- Predator/prey relationships explicit.
- Hybridization between species under specific substance conditions.

V1 is *already vastly more interesting than rare-drop loot.* V2 is
when the **real evolution** that the user wants emerges. V3 is
late-development polish.
